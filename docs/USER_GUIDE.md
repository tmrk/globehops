# GlobeHops user guide

Everything [GlobeHops](https://globehops.marki.im/) can do, how to use it and how its numbers are worked out. The [README](../README.md) has the short version; [what's new](../RECENT_CHANGES.md) lists every release.

## Install as an app

GlobeHops runs in any modern browser on phones, tablets and desktops, with nothing to install and no account to create. You can also install it as an app (your browser's Install or Add to Home Screen): it then works offline after the first visit and checks for updates whenever it opens. When an update is ready, tap **Update**, or keep exploring and let it update itself after all app windows close. The version you are using is shown in About (ⓘ) and, on larger screens, faintly in the bottom-left corner.

GlobeHops used to live at `tmrk.github.io/globehops`, which no longer works. To open an old link, replace `tmrk.github.io` with `globehops.marki.im` and keep the rest: `https://globehops.marki.im/globehops/?route=…` opens the same journey. Preferences and remembered journeys are kept per address, so choose your settings once more on the new one, and reinstall an installed app from it.

## What you can do

GlobeHops opens on the globe as it is right now, lit by the Sun, with its live clock beneath. Tap **Show a flight** to choose any two airports, or one of three long-haul ideas from a likely home airport. GlobeHops uses your browser’s time zone as a country hint, entirely on your device, and falls back to London when no gateway can be found. Each idea is a single flight to a major international airport; durations are estimates, not airline schedules.

- **Find airports** by city, airport name, IATA or ICAO code, or alternative names. Accents are optional: “malmo” finds Malmö. Hungarian and Swedish city names work too when using those languages, alongside the original names; airport names stay untranslated.
- **Plan up to ten stops.** Choose any airport to change it and use “Add stop” to continue the journey. Tap **Edit** to insert, remove or reorder stops (drag or use the keyboard) or reverse the whole journey. The reset button beside it starts over in one step, after you confirm: every stop, time and compared journey goes, and your display settings stay.
- **Explore the globe.** Drag to rotate and scroll or pinch to zoom; arrow keys and +/− work too. Select a flight in the list or on the globe to fly to it. Routes too wide for one view can be stepped through flight by flight. **Country names** and **Oceans and seas** in the display menu (the layers button at the top right) add labels in your chosen language, each on its own; more appear as you zoom in. **My location** shows where you are as a blue dot (your browser asks first). The language button beside it switches language, and the button below them re-centres the globe on your route. On phones, the journey sheet rests at three heights: lowered to show the whole globe with Play beneath it, halfway to see the journey beside the globe, or lifted to nearly the whole screen for editing (it rises by itself when you tap **Edit** or add times, and settles back when you're done).
- **Avoid restricted airspace.** Flights curve around conflict zones and closed airspace as an EU airline's would – around Russia between Europe and East Asia, for example. The zones come from EASA's conflict-zone bulletins valid on the flight's date (or today), and the extra distance lengthens the estimated flight time. A small mark beside the distance shows a detour; the flight's details say which countries it avoids and by how much. A flight that starts or ends in such a zone, or has no sensible way around, keeps its direct route with a warning. Switch it off in the display menu to see plain great circles again.
- **Stay within reach of diversion airports.** Twin-engine airliners must stay within a set flying time of an airport they could divert to on one engine (ETOPS/EDTO rules). With **Diversion-aware routing** on, long oceanic flights keep within 180 minutes of a potential diversion airport, assuming a modern twin at about 400 knots on one engine (roughly 2,220 km in still air). Most routes are unchanged; a few, such as Johannesburg–Perth, bend towards island airports, and the extra distance lengthens the estimate. Where no sensible route stays within reach, as across the South Pacific between Sydney and Santiago, the direct route stays and the flight's details say how far beyond 180 minutes it goes (airlines fly such routes with longer approvals). Switch it off in the display menu.
- **Set layovers.** Each connection starts at 1h 30m. Change it under the connecting airport, from 30 minutes to three days.
- **Show or hide country borders** in the display menu. They are on by default, with a fainter outline along every coast so land stays easy to read on the night side.
- **Add flight times (optional).** Tap “Add times” on any flight to set its departure and arrival date and time, always in each airport’s local time, with the airport’s time zone shown beside it. Times you leave out are estimated from the ones you enter and shown in place, marked with ≈; tap one to adjust it. GlobeHops warns about impossible or contradictory schedules, a clock time that occurs twice when clocks go back (you choose which one), and a time that doesn’t exist when clocks go forward.
- **Play the journey.** The amber **Play** sign beneath the globe (at the top of the journey sheet on phones) sits beside the route and its length, over a miniature of the timeline it opens. A shared link with flight times opens the timeline straight away, led by the same amber sign, which reads **Pause** while the journey plays. Press it, or Space on a keyboard, to watch the plane fly every leg and wait out each connection, with the whole trip compressed into about 15 seconds. While it plays, everything else steps aside so the globe fills the screen, with just a small player at the bottom; **Show controls** (or Escape) brings back the full timeline, and pausing brings back everything. Pause, drag the timeline to any moment, jump between flights or pick a fixed speed (0.5, 1 or 2 hours per second). The clock shows your own time, UTC or any airport’s local time.
- **Follow aircraft.** Switch on Follow aircraft in the timeline to keep the plane in view, with smooth tracking and a wider view during cruise. Drag, zoom or use the camera keys to return to manual control. Your choice is remembered and can travel in a shared link. With reduced motion, the view re-centres only as needed.
- **Day and night.** The globe is lit by the Sun for the moment shown: the current time normally, or the moment you play or scrub to. Sunlight follows the date and seasons; a narrow twilight band marks exactly where the Sun is setting or rising, and city lights come on through dusk. Turn shading off in the display menu.
- **The Sun and Moon.** With shading on, the Sun and Moon sit in space at their real positions and true apparent sizes, about half a degree across. Turn the globe to find them peeking past its edge, or choose **Find the Moon** in the display menu. With **Country names** on, both are labelled. The Moon shows its real phase and face with earthshine on its dark side, and turns faintly red in Earth's shadow during a lunar eclipse.
- **Any moment.** With no route, the time dial below the globe moves through the day: drag or flick its tape, scroll, or use the arrow keys, and pick a date for any day from 1900 to 2100. **Live** returns to now, its seconds ticking with your device's clock.
- **Live flights.** For a journey with real dates, **Live** in the timeline shows it as it is right now and keeps it moving in real time, with the camera following the plane. Share it and the link opens live, so friends and family can watch the flight as it happens.
- **Reduce motion** follows your system setting until you choose otherwise in the display menu. Your choice is remembered and can enable or reduce animations regardless of the system setting.
- **Switch kilometres and miles** in the display menu. The journey totals at the bottom of the panel show the total distance and number of flights on the left, and the overall journey time with its flying and layover time on the right.
- **Share the journey.** The page address always holds the current route, so you can bookmark it or send it to someone. The Share button copies it or opens your device’s share sheet. Shared links let the recipient use their own language.

### Saved trips

Use the bookmark beside **Share** to save a trip. It keeps your edits as you go. The bookmark at the top opens **Saved trips**; saved trips also appear in the empty planner. Remove a trip from the list and choose **Undo** to bring it back. Saved trips stay only on this device and are not synced.

### Opening a link in the installed app

Paste a GlobeHops link in **Saved trips**, the import box or an airport search. On a computer, you can also paste it anywhere outside a text field. On Android, share a link to GlobeHops. iPhone and iPad home-screen apps cannot receive links from Safari, so copy the link and paste it into GlobeHops.

### Round trips and multi-stage trips

Choose **Add return flight** below your stops to make the journey a trip: a return from your destination home appears in its own **Return** tab, ready for its own connections and times. Use **+** beside the tabs to add further journeys, such as Nairobi to Johannesburg before flying home, and **Edit** to rename a journey or remove it. The overview at the bottom shows each journey with its date, route and time, and the stay between them, such as “5 days in Nairobi · 15–20 Nov”. A stay is never counted as a layover: connections belong to a journey, stays sit between journeys. Until a journey has its own times, its stay is an estimate you can change (a week to begin with). **Play trip** flies one plane through the whole trip and skips each stay after a short pause, keeping the real dates, times and daylight; the timeline shows stays as short breaks. The play button on a journey's row plays that journey alone. To weigh up different ways of making the same trip, use **Compare trips** (below).

### Compare journeys

Choose **Compare journeys** below your stops to try up to four flight options. Each journey has its own colour and tab; a new option starts empty and offers a few similar routes between the same airports, such as nonstop or via a different hub, or you can choose any airports yourself. The ticket compares total time, shows how much longer each slower option takes and which arrives first. **Play all** runs every journey on one shared clock, including layovers, with a live status lane for each. Share keeps all the options together. Journeys without times use a common example departure when you play them. Give each option a name in **Edit**, such as the airline or the person flying it: to see two people's travels together, name them “Anna” and “Ben”, and their names follow them to the tabs, table, timeline and the planes on the globe.

### Compare round trips

Compare whole trips, not just the way there: with compared journeys, **Add return flights** gives every option its way home, and **Compare trips** below a round trip adds another option with the same journeys. Each trip keeps its colour and tab; **Outbound** and **Return** under the tabs switch between its journeys (switching trips keeps the journey you were looking at). A new trip's empty way back offers the way out reversed, with its connections or nonstop. The table at the bottom lines the trips up journey by journey and in total, highlighting the quickest way out, the quickest way back and the fastest trip overall. **Play all** flies every trip together on one clock and skips the days everyone spends at the destination. A trip without its own times travels on the same days as the timed ones, unless you choose its stay.

### Import a flight path

Choose **Import a flight path** in the planner, drop a file anywhere on the page or paste a route, to show a flight exactly as it was flown or planned. Installed as an app on a computer, GlobeHops can also open these files directly. GlobeHops reads GPX, KML and KMZ, GeoJSON, CSV (including Flightradar24 exports), IGC glider logs, Garmin FPL and Microsoft Flight Simulator or Little Navmap plans, and pasted flight-plan routes such as `EGLL DCT CPT UL9 STU DCT 52N020W 53N030W DCT KJFK`. A preview lists the flights it found, recorded or planned, with their distance, duration, points and anything worth knowing (a beacon it couldn't find, an airway drawn as a straight line) before you show them.

- **Recorded tracks** play with their own times and altitudes: the plane climbs, holds and descends exactly as the recording says. A log covering several flights is split where the aircraft stood on the ground; flights that connect form one journey and an open jaw starts the next journey of a trip.
- **Planned paths** follow their waypoints. Flight-plan routes resolve airports, radio beacons (VOR and NDB) and coordinates; named fixes and airways can't be looked up without proprietary navigation data, so the path runs straight past them and the preview says so. Planned cruising levels are eased from and to the ground with a typical climb and descent.
- Each flight's ends are matched to the nearest airport GlobeHops knows; the path itself is never moved. The flight's row shows a path mark, and the totals say where the path came from, how many points it has and how much longer than the great circle it is.
- **Compare with GlobeHops' route** shows your path beside GlobeHops' own route between the same airports, on one clock, or import a second file to compare two paths. Everything else works as with any journey: times, trips, follow aircraft, live, sharing.
- Files are read on your device and never uploaded. A shared link carries the path itself; a very long recording is simplified to fit in a link (Share says so), and **Download path** saves every point as GeoJSON to send instead.

### Map your booking with AI

No need to type a booking in. Choose **Map your booking with AI** in the planner, copy the short prompt and paste it into ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude or any other assistant together with your confirmation email, PDF or screenshot. The assistant follows GlobeHops's published [guide for AI assistants](https://globehops.marki.im/llms.txt) and replies with a link that opens your flights, with their real times, connections and return, on the globe. Your booking goes only to the assistant you choose; GlobeHops never sees it.

### Choose your language

GlobeHops speaks **English, Magyar and Svenska**. It chooses automatically using your country (inferred from the browser’s time zone) and browser languages; this is a rough country hint, not your physical location. Change language with the language button or in About (ⓘ) and your choice is remembered. You can also open [English (`/en/`)](https://globehops.marki.im/en/), [Magyar (`/hu/`)](https://globehops.marki.im/hu/) or [Svenska (`/sv/`)](https://globehops.marki.im/sv/), or use `?lang=en`, `?lang=hu` or `?lang=sv`. An explicit language address wins over your saved choice.

Dates, numbers, country labels and available city names follow the selected language. City-name coverage is incomplete (automatic matching covers about 54% of scheduled-service airports); other cities keep their original names.

### Route links

Routes use airport codes separated by hyphens: [`?route=CPH-LHR-JFK`](https://globehops.marki.im/?route=CPH-LHR-JFK). A layover different from the default follows its airport in minutes, e.g. [`?route=LHR-DXB_240-SIN-SYD`](https://globehops.marki.im/?route=LHR-DXB_240-SIN-SYD) for four hours in Dubai. Flight times are added as local times per airport, numbered by position in the route: `dep1` leaves the first airport, `arr2` reaches the second, and so on, e.g. [`?route=CPH-LHR-JFK&dep1=2026-11-20T08:15&arr3=2026-11-20T13:50`](https://globehops.marki.im/?route=CPH-LHR-JFK&dep1=2026-11-20T08:15&arr3=2026-11-20T13:50). A trip's later journeys use numbered prefixes, with an optional stay before a journey without times: [`?route=CPH-AMS-NBO&dep1=2026-11-15T07:00&2.route=NBO-CDG-CPH&2.dep1=2026-11-20T23:55`](https://globehops.marki.im/?route=CPH-AMS-NBO&dep1=2026-11-15T07:00&2.route=NBO-CDG-CPH&2.dep1=2026-11-20T23:55), or `2.stay=5d`. Compared trips add each option's later journeys the same way: `b.2.route=JFK-BUD` is option B's way home. Unknown codes are reported and skipped. An imported path travels with its flight as `trk1` (the flight leaving the first airport), `trk2` and so on, in a compact encoding. Without a route in the address, GlobeHops reopens your last journey in that browser. Links can also open live (`live=1`), request aircraft follow (`follow=1`), place and water names (`names=1`), borders (`borders=0` to hide them), routing (`airspace=0`, `diversion=0`) or a selected flight (`leg=2` for the second flight, counted across a trip). These link choices apply to that visit without changing your saved display preferences.

### For AI assistants and agents

GlobeHops publishes a documented, versioned link contract so assistants can build a working visualisation for you: [llms.txt](https://globehops.marki.im/llms.txt), [capabilities](https://globehops.marki.im/agent/capabilities.json), the [itinerary JSON Schema](https://globehops.marki.im/agent/itinerary.schema.json) and [worked examples](https://globehops.marki.im/agent/examples.json). Agent links can request a language, map layers and a selected flight as well as schedules, round trips and multi-stage trips with explicit stays, and playback. They can carry a flight's own path (planned or recorded, with altitudes and times), and an `import_route` tool turns a GPX, KML, CSV or other flight file, or a flight-plan route, into a ready link. Agents can also ask for routes around restricted airspace and within reach of diversion airports; their results keep great-circle and routed distances apart. A local [MCP](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/) server offers airport search, calculation, validation, visualisation links with a human summary, and journey timelines, plus four reference resources and a `plan_and_visualize` prompt. In browsers exposing experimental WebMCP, agents can read or edit the live journey, change its view, select a flight and obtain a share link. Agent results remain in English; calculations use the same code as the website. See [agent integration](AGENT_INTEGRATION.md).

## How the numbers are worked out

- **Distance** is the shortest great-circle path on a spherical Earth (mean radius 6,371.0088 km), or, with **Avoid restricted airspace** or **Diversion-aware routing** on, the length of a short, smooth route that respects both. Real flights also deviate because of winds, oceanic track systems, air-traffic control, weather and airways.
- **Restricted airspace** follows a small, reviewed list of [EASA conflict zone bulletins](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/domains/air-operations/czibs) (as of 9 November 2026), plus Belarus (an EASA safety directive) and Russia's closure of its airspace to EU airlines. Each zone counts only between its published dates, only for the airlines it addresses (GlobeHops assumes an EU airline because it doesn't know yours) and, for low-level advisories, only for flights too short to climb above them. Caution-only advisories and areas that cannot be located from published data are not avoided. Routes are approximate and illustrative, not flight planning; the [airspace notes](../data/AIRSPACE.md) list every zone, source and simplification.
- **Diversion airports** are open large and medium airports with a paved runway at least 2,000 m long and 45 m wide in OurAirports data (about 2,600 worldwide, including remote islands such as Midway, Easter Island and the Cocos Islands); a flight's own airports count too. Runway data cannot tell whether an airport has fire cover, opening hours, suitable weather or permission to land (several are military), so this shows modelled reach, not an airline's approved alternates or an ETOPS authorisation. The 180-minute limit and one-engine speed are representative assumptions for a modern long-haul twin; aircraft and airlines differ, and winds and published oceanic tracks are not modelled. See the [diversion notes](../data/DIVERSION.md).
- **Flight time** is an estimate, not a schedule: distance at an average 850 km/h plus 30 minutes for take-off, climb, approach and landing, rounded to five minutes.
- **Total time** adds the layover at each connection to the flight times. When you enter times, your times win and the totals follow them. In a trip, stays sit between journeys and are shown separately.
- **Time zones** come from each airport’s location (IANA time zones), so daylight saving time, historical changes and the International Date Line are handled. Future dates use today’s time-zone rules, which governments can change.
- **The plane’s position** during playback is an estimate: it moves along the great circle (or the detour) at a pace that follows the simplified climb, cruise and descent profile. It is not a real flight track – unless you import one: a recorded track places the plane by its own timestamps and altitudes, and a planned path is flown along its waypoints at the estimated pace.
- **Day and night** follow the Sun’s position for the moment shown, calculated with a standard astronomical algorithm accurate to a few hundredths of a degree. Twilight brightness is illustrative; weather, terrain and local refraction can change observed sunrise and sunset. The city lights are a 2016 satellite composite, not live.
- **The arcs** follow a simplified flight profile. Each flight climbs to a typical cruising altitude of about 11 km over roughly 20 minutes, holds it, and descends over about the last 25 minutes. Flights too short to reach cruise peak lower. Height is exaggerated 12× so it is visible on the globe. Seen from above, each arc follows its ground track exactly.
